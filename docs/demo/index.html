FROM THOUGHTS TO A PLAN

How to time block your day with Time Boxed

Time blocking means giving a task or activity a specific place on your schedule. A time box sets a start and end time, helping you decide what fits in your day.

  1. Capture your thoughts. Put loose tasks and ideas in the Brain Dump, then choose your top priorities.
  2. Make space on your timeline. Choose your planning hours and 30- or 60-minute slots. Merge adjacent blocks when a task needs more time.
  3. Adjust as your day unfolds. Edit your blocks as plans change. The iOS app saves your day locally, even when you’re offline.

A FEW THINGS TO KNOW

Time Boxed questions, answered

Is Time Boxed free?

Yes. Today’s priorities, Brain Dump, and time boxes are free, including custom planning hours, block lengths, and merging adjacent blocks. Optional Time Boxed Pro adds saved-day history and exports to Apple Calendar and Reminders. Pro offers a monthly subscription or a one-time lifetime purchase; see the App Store listing for current local pricing.

Does Time Boxed work offline or require an account?

The iPhone and iPad app saves plans locally on your device and works offline. No app account is required. Your planner content is not uploaded to a developer-operated server. Read the privacy policy for details about purchases and optional exports.

Which devices does Time Boxed support?

Time Boxed is a native app for iPhone with iOS 17 or later and iPad with iPadOS 17 or later. This page provides a browser demo of the daily planner.

Can I export my time blocks to Calendar or Reminders?

Yes, with Time Boxed Pro. Export a filled block to Apple Calendar with its start and end times, or to Apple Reminders using its start time as the due date. The app requests permission when you choose to export.

Does the web demo save or sync my plans?

The interactive demo keeps edits only in the current page. Resetting or reloading restores the sample day. Demo edits do not sync to the iOS app. The iOS app saves its plans locally on your device.

Who makes Time Boxed, and is it open source?

Time Boxed is developed by Greg Adams and is open source under the MIT License. You can inspect the code, report issues, or contribute through the Time Boxed GitHub repository. Accepted and merged contributions qualify for free lifetime Pro; see the contribution guide for the reward terms.
